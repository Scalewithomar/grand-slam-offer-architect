# Offer Creation: Problems → Solutions → Delivery → Trim & Stack

Sources: *$100M Offers* Section III, Ch. 8–10 (The Thought Process; Creating Your
Grand Slam Offer Parts I & II). This is the engine behind Parts 4–7.

## Convergent vs divergent thinking

- **Convergent** (school math): many known variables, one right answer. Binary.
- **Divergent** (what life pays for): multiple variables, known & unknown, dynamic
  conditions, **many right answers** — one far more right than the rest.

Offer-building requires **divergent** thinking. The **Brick Exercise**: in 120
seconds, list every possible use of a brick. Most people list a handful; then
realize the brick could be different sizes, materials (gold, plastic, wood),
shapes (holes, interlocking) — unlocking far more uses. Lesson: every offer has
building blocks; use divergent thinking to combine them into value in many ways.

## The five-step create process

### Step 1 — Identify the Dream Outcome  *(Part 3)*
Sell the destination, not the vehicle. "Lose 20 lbs in 6 weeks" = big dream
outcome (lose 20 lbs) + decreased time delay (6 weeks). Not "a membership" — the
result they'll experience on arrival.

### Step 2 — List Problems  *(Part 4)*
Write down everything the buyer struggles with, in the **sequence they'll hit it**,
in **insane detail**. For each core action, ask what happens *immediately before*
and *after* using your product — "what's the next thing they need help with?"

Every problem tends to carry **all four negative elements**, aligned to the value
drivers:
1. **Dream Outcome** → "this won't be financially worth it."
2. **Likelihood** → "it won't work for me specifically / I can't stick with it /
   external factors will get in my way." *(the most service-specific bucket)*
3. **Effort & Sacrifice** → "too hard, confusing, I'll suck at it, I won't like it."
4. **Time** → "takes too long / I'm too busy / won't be convenient."

Example (weight loss): "Buying healthy food" → hard & confusing / takes too much
time / too expensive / unsustainable / family gets in the way / undoable when
traveling. Repeat for cooking, eating, exercising, staying social, etc. This
yields dozens of problems — **good news: more problems = more you get to solve.**

### Step 3 — Solutions List  *(Part 5)*
Turn each problem into a solution by asking "what would I need to show someone to
solve this?" Reverse the problem into **"how to…"** language:
- "buying healthy food is hard" → *How to make buying healthy food easy and
  enjoyable so anyone can do it.*
- "takes too much time" → *How to buy healthy food quickly.*
- "is expensive" → *How to buy healthy food for less than your current bill.*

**You must solve *every* perceived problem.** One unaddressed "one thing" can kill
a sale (the eating-out-guide anecdote: adding one missing solution closed a lost
sale, and became a permanent asset). Don't get romantic about *how* you solve it —
find a way to solve each one. Solving all of them converts the most people at the
highest price and margin.

### Step 4 — Create the Delivery Vehicles ("The How")  *(Part 6)*
Brainstorm *every* way you could deliver each solution — push past what you'd
normally default to (divergent, high-leverage; you only do this once per product).
Group by how many you serve at once. **Delivery Cube cheat codes:**

1. **Personal attention:** one-on-one · small group · one-to-many.
2. **Effort expected from them:** **DIY** (they do it) · **DWY** (you do it with
   them) · **DFY** (you do it for them).
3. **Live medium:** in-person · phone · email · text · Zoom · chat.
4. **Recorded format:** audio · video · written.
5. **Response speed / hours:** 24/7 · 9–5 · within 5 min · within 1 hr · within 24
   hr; which days.
6. **The 10x-to-1/10th test:** *If they paid 10× (or $100,000), what would you
   provide? If they paid 1/10th and you still had to make them succeed, how would
   you do it?* Stretching both directions surfaces radically different solutions.

Prefer **high-value one-to-many** assets (checklists, calculators, recorded tours,
templates, tools, software): high one-time cost of creation, near-zero marginal
cost, value in perpetuity. Save one-on-one / small-group for the biggest value adds
only.

### Step 5 — Trim & Stack  *(Part 7)*

**5a. Trim.** List the cost-to-you of each candidate solution. Remove **high-cost,
low-value** first; then remove **low-cost, low-value**. Keep only **low-cost,
high-value** and **high-cost, high-value**. To judge value, run each through the
Value Equation: will the buyer *financially value* it, *believe they'll succeed*,
feel *less effort/sacrifice*, and get it with *less time*? For any dream deliverable
that's too costly (e.g. "move in with them"), step back one notch at a time until
the cost/time is acceptable — or raise the price so it's worth it.

**5b. Stack.** Bundle the survivors into one high-value deliverable. Present each
as **Problem → Solution wording → sexier named bundle** with a value tag, e.g.
"Foolproof Bargain Grocery System … $1,000 value." Sum the values (e.g. total
$4,351 value) against one price (e.g. $599).

A great bundle does three things:
1. Solves **all** perceived problems (not just some).
2. Gives you conviction it's **one of a kind.**
3. Makes it **impossible to compare** to the business down the street.

The result: prospects make a **value-based**, not price-based, decision — no longer
bound by commoditization.

## The Sales-to-Fulfillment Continuum

There's a trade-off between **ease of sales** and **ease of fulfillment**: do more
for the client → easier to sell, harder to fulfill; do less → harder to sell,
easier to fulfill. Aim for the sweet spot: sells well *and* fulfills easily.

**Mantra: "Create flow. Monetize flow. Then add friction."** Generate demand
first; get people saying yes; *then* optimize/reduce what you do. For a first Grand
Slam Offer, **over-deliver like crazy** — get them thinking "I get all this, for
only that?" — then use the cash flow to build systems that deliver the same value
at lower cost. Everyone should get a bargain: high price, but a steal for the value.
