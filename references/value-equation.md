# The Value Equation

The single most important framework for making an offer worth more. Source:
*$100M Offers*, Section III, Ch. 6. Use it in Parts 3, 4, 5, 8, and 11.

> **Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)**

Four primary drivers of value. **Increase the top two. Decrease the bottom two.**

| # | Driver | Goal | The buyer's silent question |
|---|--------|------|------------------------------|
| 1 | Dream Outcome | **Increase** | "What will I make / become?" |
| 2 | Perceived Likelihood of Achievement | **Increase** | "How do I know it will work *for me*?" |
| 3 | Time Delay | **Decrease** | "How long until I get it?" |
| 4 | Effort & Sacrifice | **Decrease** | "What is expected of me?" |

## Why it's a division, not a sum

If you drive the **bottom to zero** — zero time delay, zero effort — value
becomes *infinite* (anything ÷ 0). The hypothetical limit: the moment the card is
charged, the dream outcome becomes reality with no work. Strive toward that.

**Beginners over-invest in the top** (bigger and bigger claims — easy, lazy, and
easily copied, so less unique). **The best companies attack the bottom** — making
things immediate, seamless, effortless (Apple, Amazon 1-click, Netflix). The
bottom is harder and more competitive, so winning there is rewarded more.

## Perception is reality

None of this is valuable until the prospect **perceives** it. Your job is to
communicate each driver with clarity so the prospect *feels* the higher
likelihood, the shorter time, the lower effort. (London Underground: a cheap
dotted map showing the next train's arrival raised rider satisfaction more than
actually running faster trains — it cut *perceived* wait.)

**Logical vs psychological solutions:** logical solutions have usually already
been tried (that's why they're obvious). Reach for psychological ones. *"Any fool
can sell a product at a discount; it takes great marketing to sell the same
product at a premium."* — after Rory Sutherland.

## Driver 1 — Dream Outcome (increase)

The gap between the buyer's current reality and their dreams. Don't *create*
desire — **channel** existing desire through your vehicle. Depict the dream
accurately so the buyer feels understood, then show how your vehicle gets them
there. Remember: **sell the vacation, not the flight.**

Deep human desires people pay for: to be **beautiful, respected, powerful, loved,
higher in status.** In practice, the outcome that most increases the buyer's
**status** (relative standing vs others) is valued most. Frame benefits in terms
of how *others* will perceive the buyer's win ("your golf buddies' jaws will
drop…").

When two offers satisfy the *same* desire, the Dream Outcome cancels out — the
other three drivers decide the price. (What makes one "become beautiful" offer
$50,000 and another $5? The other three variables.)

## Driver 2 — Perceived Likelihood of Achievement (increase)

**People pay for certainty.** "How likely is it that I'll get the result if I
buy?" The plastic surgeon's 10,000th patient can charge far more than for their
first — same procedure, higher perceived likelihood. Raise it with: proof and
testimonials, track record, guarantees/risk reversal, what you include vs
exclude, and precise messaging. This is the **most service-specific** of the
belief barriers ("it won't work for *me* / I can't stick with it / external
factors will get in my way").

## Driver 3 — Time Delay (decrease)

Time between buying and receiving the promised benefit. Shorter = more valuable.
Two elements:
- **Long-term outcome** — the thing they *buy* (the dream).
- **Short-term experience** — the small early wins that make them *stay* long
  enough to get it.

Engineer a **big, fast emotional win** as close to purchase as possible (it
builds trust and momentum; backed by science — early victories increase
follow-through). Example: get a new gym client their first $2,000 sale in 7 days.

> **Fast beats free.** The only thing that beats "free" is "fast." People pay for
> speed (MVD vs DMV, FedEx vs USPS, Uber vs walking). If you compete against free,
> double down on speed.

## Driver 4 — Effort & Sacrifice (decrease)

The ancillary costs — tangible and intangible — the buyer pays "along the way."
This is why **DFY > DWY > DIY** in price (less effort, plus higher perceived
likelihood since an expert does it). Fitness vs liposuction: same outcome (lose
fat), wildly different price, because time delay, effort, and perceived
likelihood differ. Reducing *perceived* effort/sacrifice massively boosts appeal.

## Putting it together — worked contrasts

Rate each driver 0/1 and compare vehicles with the *same* dream outcome:

- **Meditation vs Xanax** ("relaxation, less anxiety, well-being"): Xanax wins on
  time delay and effort → Xanax is a multi-billion-dollar product; meditation
  businesses rarely are. Same outcome, different value.
- **Supplements ($123B) vs health clubs ($62B):** same objectives, but a pill is
  faster and easier than daily gym visits → perceived as more valuable.

Lesson: monetize what people **value** (not merely what's good for them), then
use that to deliver what they need. Win-win.

## How to use the equation in the workflow

- **Part 3 (Dream Outcome):** maximize driver 1; make it measurable + time-bound.
- **Part 4 (Obstacles):** every obstacle maps to a driver — "not worth it"
  (Dream Outcome), "won't work for me" (Likelihood), "too hard/confusing"
  (Effort), "too slow/too busy" (Time). Use the four as prompts to surface
  obstacles.
- **Part 5–7 (Solutions/Delivery/Stack):** score each candidate deliverable —
  does it raise likelihood, cut time, or cut effort? Keep high-value items.
- **Part 8 (Proof):** proof is the primary lever on driver 2.
- **Part 11 (HSO):** the Story must dismantle the belief barrier (driver 2), not
  list features.
