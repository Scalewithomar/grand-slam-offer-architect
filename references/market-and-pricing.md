# Pricing & Growth Economics

Sources: *$100M Offers* Section II (The Commodity Problem, Charge What It's
Worth). Use in Parts 1, 9, 10 — and whenever the user wants to raise prices or
justify value.

## The three (really two) ways to grow

There are only three ways to grow any business:
1. **Get more customers**
2. **Increase their average purchase value** (profit per purchase)
3. **Get them to buy more times** (frequency)

Simplified, that's two: *get more customers* and *increase each customer's value*.
A Grand Slam Offer helps with **all three** because it lets you sell on **value,
not price**.

> Max revenue = (new customers per period) × (lifetime value). 10 clients/mo ×
> $1,000 LTV = $10,000/mo ceiling. To grow, raise either factor.

## The Commodity Problem

**Commoditized = price-driven purchases = a race to the bottom.** When every
competitor offers "a little more for a little less," the market reaches "perfect
efficiency" and everyone just barely survives. If a buyer can find a cheaper
version of the "same thing," the value discrepancy makes them switch. Margins
vanish.

**Differentiated = value-driven purchases = a category of one.** Make the offer so
different it can't be compared — then you re-calibrate the buyer's value-meter and
escape price competition. You become a "monopoly" that can make "monopoly profits."

**Money math (real lead-gen agency example):**
- *Old commoditized way:* $1,000 down + $1,000/mo retainer. At 0.5–1× ROAS you
  *lose* money acquiring customers and only break even later.
- *New Grand Slam Offer way:* pay once, cover ad spend, pay-on-performance, "20
  clients in month one or next month free," + the full playbook free. Result:
  ~2.5× more responses × ~2.5× more closes × ~4× higher price ≈ **22.4× more cash
  collected up front** ($10k spent → ~$112k). You make money *acquiring* customers.

## Price-to-Value Discrepancy

People buy to get a **deal** — they believe VALUE received > PRICE paid. The moment
value dips below price, they stop buying. *"Price is what you pay; value is what you
get."* (Buffett)

Lowering price is the easy way to widen the gap — and usually the **wrong** one:
you can only go down to $0 but up infinitely. *"There is no strategic benefit to
being the second cheapest in the marketplace, but there is for being the most
expensive."* (Dan Kennedy) **Goal: get more people to say yes at a higher price by
increasing the value-to-price discrepancy** — sell $100,000 of value for $10,000
("money at a discount").

## The Virtuous Cycle of Price

**When you lower price you:** decrease the client's emotional investment → decrease
perceived value ("can't be good if it's this cheap") → decrease their results
(uninvested) → attract the worst, never-satisfied clients → destroy the margin you
need to deliver an exceptional experience. Your world (and your service) gets worse.

**When you raise price you:** increase emotional investment → increase perceived
value → increase results (they're invested) → attract the **best** clients (easiest
to satisfy, cheapest to fulfill) → multiply margin to reinvest in systems, people,
experience, and scale.

**Higher price literally means higher value** (blind wine tasting: identical wine
rated best when priced highest). Don't aim to be *slightly* above market — be far
enough above that the buyer thinks "there must be something entirely different
going on here." **Those who pay the most pay the most attention** → adhere more →
get better results → so premium pricing is, in a real sense, the *moral* choice if
you care about outcomes. But your product must deliver; conviction comes from
having done it many times (outwork your self-doubt).

**Premium proof point (Gym Launch):** entered a market of $500/mo competitors and
one $5,000 competitor; priced at **$16,000** for a 16-week done-with-you intensive
(3× the top player, 32× the low players), then upsold ~35% into $42,000/yr. Justified
by real average results at 11 months: +$239k/yr top-line, +$160k/yr recurring,
profit 3.1×, +67 clients, churn 10.7%→6.8%. *99% of businesses need to raise prices
to grow, not lower them. Profit is oxygen.*

## Offer levels (Part 1)

- **Entry** — low price, used to acquire new customers.
- **Core** — the main current or target offer.
- **High Ticket** — premium price, bigger result.
- **Continuity** — subscription / retainer.

Match delivery model, guarantee type, and funnel objective to the level. High
ticket + high fulfillment cost → prefer conditional or anti-guarantees (see
`offer-enhancers.md`).

## Key definitions

- **Gross Profit** — revenue minus the *direct* cost of servicing one additional
  customer. ($10 lotion costing $2 → $8 / 80%.) Not net profit.
- **LTV (Lifetime Value / LTGP)** — gross profit over a customer's lifetime =
  gross profit per purchase × number of purchases. ($1,000/mo × 90% margin × 5
  months = $4,500.) Hormozi tracks gross-profit LTV, not revenue LTV.
- **Value-Driven vs Price-Driven** — differentiate → value-driven (category of
  one); commoditize → price-driven (race to the bottom).
