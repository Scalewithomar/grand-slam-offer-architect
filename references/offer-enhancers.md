# Enhancing the Offer: Scarcity · Urgency · Bonuses · Guarantees · Naming

Source: *$100M Offers* Section IV. These five levers make an offer more desirable
**without changing the core offer.** Use in Parts 7, 9, 10, 11.

**The Delicate Dance of Desire.** All marketing shifts the supply/demand curve:
increase demand, decrease (perceived) supply → sell more units *and* for more.
Desire comes from *not* getting what you want — so keep supply/satisfaction *under*
the demand you can generate.
> **Hormozi's Law:** "The longer you delay the ask, the bigger the ask you can
> make. The longer the runway, the bigger the plane that can take off."

The five levers: **Scarcity** (cut supply → raise price + exclusivity), **Urgency**
(cut the action threshold via time), **Bonuses** (raise demand + perceived value),
**Guarantees** (reverse risk), **Naming** (re-stimulate demand, expand awareness).

---

## 1. Scarcity — a function of *quantity*

Fixed/limited supply creates fear of missing out. **Fear of loss > desire for
gain.** Three broad types:
1. **Limited supply of seats/slots** (in general or per period).
2. **Limited supply of bonuses.**
3. **Never available again.**

**Physical products:** limited releases (flavors, colors, designs, sizes). *Always
sell out* — better to under-supply and sell out than over-order and fail to create
scarcity. **Announce that you sold out** (social proof + they missed it → want it
more next time). Repeat, but not too often (~once a month is the sweet spot).
Chanel sends only 1–2 of each piece per store — every item is the last or
second-to-last in stock.

**Services** (three ethical mechanisms):
1. **Total Business Cap** — "only ever service X clients." Waiting list; open slots
   periodically (raise 10–20% then re-cap). Best for top tiers.
2. **Growth-Rate Cap** — "only X new clients per week." "We take 5/week, 3 spots
   gone; take this one or wait until we reopen."
3. **Cohort Cap** — "X clients per class/cohort," on whatever cadence (monthly,
   quarterly): "We take 100 clients 4× a year — doors open, then close."

**Honest Scarcity (the most ethical):** you genuinely can't take 1,000 clients
tomorrow. Pick a real capacity number and advertise your progress toward it: "81%
to capacity this week" both nudges buyers *and* implies social proof. Only you
decide where "full" is.

**Pro tips:** scarcity works especially well on higher-ticket upsells (workshops,
events, consulting). **Extreme scarcity:** sell a tiny, high-priced tier of
1-on-1 access (DM/email/voice/Zoom) — some of your best clients. **"Once you're
out, you can never come back"** makes people think hard about leaving (works best
with small groups).

## 2. Urgency — a function of *time*

Limit *when* people can sign up (vs how many). Deadlines drive decisions. Four
ethical methods:

1. **Cohort-Based Rolling Urgency** — "sign up today and start with Monday's group,
   or wait for the next kickoff." Optional nudge: "a client just dropped, so I have
   one opening for Monday." Less-frequent kickoffs = stronger.
2. **Rolling Seasonal Urgency** — real countdowns / dated promotions. Same core
   offer, new "wrapper" per season ("New Year Promo ends Jan 30," "Sexy by Spring
   ends March 31"). **#1 strategy for local businesses** (fresh dated wrapper on the
   same service beats "same old" campaigns). Dates must be real.
3. **Pricing / Bonus-Based Urgency** — the *promotion, discount, or bonus* is what
   they miss, not the service — lets year-round sellers use urgency honestly ("not
   sure how long we'll run this; we change them every ~4 weeks"). Also: **clean your
   pipeline with every price change** — "price is going up, get in now."
4. **Exploding Opportunity** — the opportunity itself decays with time (arbitrage,
   early platform access, exploding job offers). Every delay = missed gains.

**The fear is unfounded.** On a week-long launch, **50–60% of sales land in the
last few hours of the last day.** You make more from the many who act than you lose
from the few who "miss out" (they were never going to buy anyway).

## 3. Bonuses

**A single offer is less valuable than the same offer broken into components and
stacked as bonuses.** Anchor the price to the core offer, then each added bonus
widens the price-to-value discrepancy until it's too big to bear. (Infomercials:
"but wait, there's more!" — establish price, then pile on until it'd be stupid to
pass.) **Add bonuses instead of discounting** — discounting teaches buyers your
price is negotiable and signals weakness; bonuses add value from a position of
strength.

**1-on-1 vs group:** selling 1-on-1, **ask for the sale first**; reveal bonuses
*after* they say yes (a "wow" that reinforces the decision). If they say no, add a
bonus that matches their specific objection, then ask again ("fair enough?").

### The 11 Bonus Bullets (how to present each bonus)
1. **Always offer them** (use the stacked bundle from Trim & Stack).
2. Give each a **special name with a benefit in the title.**
3. Tell them: (a) how it relates to their problem, (b) what it is, (c) how you
   discovered/created it, (d) how it improves their life — faster/easier/less
   effort (Value Equation).
4. Provide **proof** it's valuable (stat, past client, personal experience).
5. **Paint a vivid mental picture** of life already using it.
6. **Always ascribe a price tag** and justify it.
7. **Tools & checklists beat additional trainings** (lower effort/time → higher
   value).
8. Each should crush a **specific obstacle/concern** (prove their limiting belief
   wrong).
9. Solve their **next** problem before they hit it.
10. The **bonuses' total value should eclipse the core offer's** (implies the core
    must be even more valuable).
11. **Add scarcity/urgency to the bonuses** ("only through this program," "buy
    today or lose this $1,000 bonus").

### Advanced: Other People's Products & Services (OPP bonuses)
Get non-competing businesses to give their products/services as your bonuses in
exchange for free exposure to your customers. Their free marketing = your
high-value, no-cost bonuses. (Pain clinic: free massages, chiro adjustments,
food/supplement discounts, gym sessions — bonus value can exceed your entire
price.) Go further: negotiate a **group discount + referral commission** so each
bonus becomes an indirect *and* direct revenue stream.

**What should be a bonus vs core?** The **wow-factor** pieces — distinct, high
value, short but potent (checklists, infographics, maps) that could get lost inside
the core. Pull them out and spotlight them. Information products are ideal (high
perceived value, low cost, zero operational effort). Build a **vault** of these
assets over time.

## 4. Guarantees (Risk Reversal)

**Risk is the #1 objection.** Reversing it can 2–4× conversion (Fladlien). Always
state a guarantee **boldly with a reason why**, even a modest one.

**A guarantee needs teeth — a conditional statement:**
> **"If you don't get [X result] in [Y time], we will [Z]."**
Without the "or what" (Z), it's weak. *Bad:* "We'll get you 20 clients guaranteed."
*Good:* "You'll get 20 clients in 30 days, or we refund your money **plus** your ad
spend."

**Do the math, don't be emotional.** 100 sales/5% refund = 95 net. A stronger
guarantee: 130 sales/10% refund = 117 net = **1.23× (23% more)**. For a guarantee to
lose money, the extra refunds would have to fully offset the extra sales — unlikely.
**Warning:** buyers who buy *only* because of a guarantee can be poor customers →
tie the guarantee to the actions required for success. High fulfillment cost →
prefer **conditional** or **anti-**guarantees (you'd eat refund *and* delivery cost).

### The four types
1. **Unconditional** — strongest for volume; a "pay first, then decide" trial.
   Drives the most buyers but the most refunds. Best for lower-ticket B2C.
2. **Conditional** — has terms tied to the **key actions** for success. Most
   creative; aim for "better than money back." Ideally 100% would qualify but have
   already gotten the result, so they don't claim it. (Given the choice of refund vs
   the promised outcome, most take the outcome.)
3. **Anti-Guarantee** — "all sales are final," with a strong "reason why" (a
   damaging admission of exposure: "once you see this, it can't be unseen"). For
   consumables or things that lose value once revealed; and for high-ticket work
   ("if you need a guarantee to jump, you're not who we want").
4. **Implied / Performance** — revshare, profit-share, pay-per-result, ratchets,
   triggers, bonuses. "If I don't perform, I don't get paid" — best alignment;
   weeds out low performers. Needs transparent measurement + trust/control over
   collection. Can pair with a minimum ("greater of $1,000 or 10% of revenue") or a
   ramp ("$1,000/mo for 3 months, then 100% performance").

### Stacking guarantees
Combine an unconditional (30-day no-questions) *with* a conditional ("triple your
money back in 90 days if you do 1-2-3"). Or stack two conditional outcomes over a
timeline ("$10k by day 60, $30k by day 90, as long as you do X, Y, Z") — future-
paces the prospect into believing the outcome.

### Example library (from the book)
- **[Unconditional] No-Questions-Asked Refund** — full refund / 50% / refund of ad
  spend & ancillary costs / pay for a competitor's program / money back + extra
  $1,000. Simple, strong, risky.
- **[Unconditional] Satisfaction-Based Refund** — refund anytime they're not
  satisfied with the *service level*. Pairs with a best-case/worst-case close.
  (Author had 2 claims out of 4,000 sales.) Best at lower ticket.
- **[Conditional] Outsized Refund** — double/triple money back, or a large
  no-strings payment, gated on consumption conditions. (Fladlien: "spend $X on ads
  and don't profit → I'll buy your store for $25,000" → +$3M sales, only 10 payouts.)
- **[Conditional] Service Guarantee** — keep working free until X is achieved
  (guarantees the outcome, removes time risk). Author's personal favorite; add
  action-conditions (attend calls, report data, etc.).
- **[Conditional] Modified Service Guarantee** — free extension of service for a set
  period Y (usually ≥2× the original) rather than "forever."
- **[Conditional] Credit-Based** — refund as credit toward any service. Great during
  an upsell.
- **[Conditional] Personal Service** — you work with them 1-on-1 free until X.
  Extremely strong; add strict conditions and edification.
- **[Conditional] Hotel + Airfare Perks** — reimburse product + travel. Great for
  workshops/events; tangible.
- **[Conditional] Wage-Payment** — pay their hourly rate if the session isn't
  valuable (they'd have to produce a tax return to claim — no one does).
- **[Conditional] Release of Service** — let them out of a contract free (voids
  commitment/cancellation fees).
- **[Conditional] Delayed Second Payment** — don't bill again until they get their
  first outcome (first sale, first 5 lbs, site live). Focuses your team on
  activation.
- **[Conditional] First Outcome** — you cover ancillary costs (ad spend, etc.) until
  their first outcome.
- **[Anti-Guarantee] All Sales Final** — for exclusive, easily-copied, high-value
  IP; lean into "it works so well and is so easy to steal that all sales are final."

**Name your guarantee** something vivid, not "30-Day Satisfaction Guarantee":
*"In 30 days, if you wouldn't jump into shark-infested waters to get our product
back, we return every dollar."*

**Create your own:** identify the client's biggest fears, pains, and perceived
obstacles ("what do they *not* want to happen if they pay you?") and **reverse them
into a guarantee.** Guarantees are *enhancers* — they can't save a bad product or
sales team (they'll just cause refunds). Good starting move: service-based or
performance guarantees (all sales final → no refund fear; commits you to results),
then move to less restrictive guarantees for more volume.

## 5. Naming — M-A-G-I-C

A great offer no one hears about earns nothing. Naming determines click-through,
outbound response, and inbound engagement. Over time offers **fatigue** (faster in
local markets) — rename to refresh; **you change only the wrapper, never the core
offer.**

**The M-A-G-I-C formula** (use **3–5** components, not always all; short & punchy;
order doesn't matter — go with what's punchier):

| Letter | Component | Purpose | Examples |
|--------|-----------|---------|----------|
| **M** | **Make a Magnetic "Reason Why"** | Attention | Free, 88% Off, Giveaway, Grand Opening, Spring, Back to School, Anniversary, New Year |
| **A** | **Announce your Avatar** | Discrimination | Bee Cave Dentists, Rolling Hills Moms, Salon Owners, Retired Athletes, Brooklyn Busy Executives (hyper-local beats broad) |
| **G** | **Give them a Goal** (dream outcome) | Purpose | Pain Free, Celebrity Smile, 1st Place, Double Your Profit, First Client, 7-Figure |
| **I** | **Indicate a time Interval** | Timeline | 4-Hour, 21-Day, 6-Week, 3-Month |
| **C** | **Complete with a Container word** | Method | Challenge, Blueprint, Bootcamp, Intensive, Incubator, Masterclass, Accelerator, Fast Track, Sprint, System, Detox, Transformation, Mastermind, Shortcut, Reset |

**Compliance note:** many ad platforms reject a quantifiable claim + a duration
(implies a guarantee). "$10,000 in 10 days" may be rejected; "Make Your First Sale
in 10 Days" is usually fine. Use interval freely where a claim isn't involved.

**Pro tips:** **rhyme** ("Six-Pack Fast Track," "5-Day Book Print Sprint") or
**alliterate** ("Make Money Masterclass," "Debt Detox") — nice-to-have, don't force
it. Name **sub-items and bonuses** with the same formula. Test 2–3 names; keep the
winner as a control and test new names against it.

**Named-offer examples:** *Free Six-Week Lean-By-Halloween Challenge · 88% Off
12-Week Bikini Blueprint · $2,000-Off Celebrity Smile Transformation · Back Sore No
More! 90-Day Rapid Healing Intensive (81% off) · 5 Clients in 5 Days Blueprint ·
Fill Your Gym in 30 Days (Free!).*

### Offer-fatigue order (change top → bottom; lighter → heavier)
1. Change the **creative** (images/video).
2. Change the **body copy.**
3. Change the **headline / wrapper** (rename the offer).
4. Vary the **season/theme** name.
5. Change the **duration.**
6. Change the **enhancer** (the free/discount component).
7. Change the **monetization structure / price points** (last resort).

Once an offer is monetized, rarely change the machine — rinse and repeat. Exhaust
the lighter changes first (they're cheaper operationally). Local markets fatigue
fastest (small TAM) — vary the wrapper often; national markets are harder to start
but scale further.

---

## Section recap
Make the core valuable (Value Equation + Trim & Stack) → add a **guarantee** to get
more people to buy and consume → present with **urgency & scarcity** to raise desire
→ **name** it to attract the right avatar and repel the wrong one, wrapped in one
big, understandable promise.
